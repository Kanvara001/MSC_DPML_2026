Tabular data

How each three hours runs

The same shape every week, so students know what to prepare.

BlockClockWhat happens
Lecture A0:00 – 1:05First half of the material. Roughly 15 slides with discussion — the deck's timer (T) keeps you honest.
Break1:05 – 1:20Coffee. The second block is always the harder one.
Lecture B1:20 – 2:10Second half, ending on the session's five takeaway sentences.
Paper clinic2:10 – 3:00Students work on a real published paper against a structured prompt, then present. Each session's clinic slide has the full run-through and the take-home task.

The long-form edition

Five chapters, 291 slides, 79 figures, mapped to a twelve-week semester — open the full course index (keep both editions in the same folder).

What was cut, and why. The condensed edition keeps every argument that changes what a student does: the grouping unit, fitting inside the fold, the imbalance–calibration trade-off, tokenizer fertility, patient- and site-level splits, the random baseline, and the subgroup table. It drops the technique catalogues that are better as reference than as lecture — contrast codings, the full outlier-detector zoo, mixing laws and sequence packing, text augmentation, self-supervised view construction, and most of the per-section reference slides. All of it survives in the long edition.

On the citations. Sources appear as numbered footnotes at the foot of the slide that cites them, with a superscript marker at the mention. They are derived from the long edition's reference lists, so nothing is invented.

On the figures. Every diagram was drawn for this course as inline vector graphics — nothing is copied from a published paper, so the decks can be shared, printed and adapted freely.

Keep a backup. Edits live in this browser's storage. Before reinstalling, changing machine or clearing site data, use Export → JSON backup in each deck.
